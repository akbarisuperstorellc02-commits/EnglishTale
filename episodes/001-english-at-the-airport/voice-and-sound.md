# Voice and Sound Production

## Voice roles

| Role | Voice direction | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Narrator | Warm, clear American English; medium pace | Neutral, welcoming, not overly theatrical |
| Leo | Young adult male; curious, slightly nervous at first | Natural conversational delivery |
| Emma | Young adult female; supportive, upbeat | Calm and reassuring |
| Airport agent | Adult; polite, professional | Clear enunciation |
| Security officer | Adult; neutral and courteous | Short, direct instructions |
| Ollie | Cheerful, friendly guide | Slightly playful, still clear |
| Persian explainer | Natural Persian; moderate pace | Keep translation concise |

## Recording / TTS workflow
1. Generate one line or short paragraph per file, not one huge audio file.
2. Keep voice settings consistent for each character throughout the series.
3. Listen to every line for mispronunciation, unnatural emphasis, and clipped endings.
4. Re-generate lines with pronunciation errors.
5. Export WAV when possible for editing; high-quality MP3 is acceptable if WAV is unavailable.
6. Keep the English line and Persian translation as separate audio clips.
7. Name files with the convention in the brand guide.

## Mix targets (practical guidance)
- Dialogue must be clearly intelligible on a phone speaker.
- Keep music well below dialogue; duck it during all spoken lines.
- Avoid clipping; leave headroom on the master.
- Use gentle noise reduction only when needed; avoid metallic artifacts.
- Add subtle airport ambience only in establishing shots and keep it quiet.
- Do not use recognizable copyrighted songs or unlicensed sound effects.

## Free / low-cost tool options
Tools and free-plan terms change frequently. Check current availability and commercial-use rights before production.
- Text drafting and translation: ChatGPT.
- Voice generation: a TTS provider with a plan/license that permits commercial YouTube use; check the specific plan terms.
- Editing: CapCut or another editor with an appropriate license.
- Music and sound effects: YouTube Audio Library or another properly licensed library.

Never assume “free to generate” means “free for commercial use.”
